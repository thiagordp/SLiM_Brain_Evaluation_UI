This page needs a more careful redesign than CITES because it combines **two different completeness questions**:

1. **Claim recall** — did the Brain extract the important Claims of the paper?
2. **Conceptual coverage** — looking across all those Claims, did the resulting mappings capture the concepts that matter for the mapping review?

The workbook clearly contains the first as Source-level **HE-19.1 + HE-19.3**. It contains **HE-20 only at Claim level**, where the evaluator asks whether the concepts engaged by an individual Claim are all mapped.  

The 7 September meeting then identified exactly the gap visible in the screenshot: Claim-level concept completeness is not enough. The discussion says that the evaluator should, **at the end, once the whole Source has been evaluated**, ask whether something relevant to the paper from the systematic-mapping perspective was not captured anywhere in its Claims/mappings. The team explicitly distinguished this from asking whether every concept that is merely *conceptually possible* could have been attached to an individual Claim. [Meeting 07-09](sandbox:/mnt/data/Meeting-07-09-Brain.srt)

So I think the **source-level conceptual coverage criterion should stay**. But it should be presented honestly as a **post-workbook calibration addition**, rather than as if it had always been part of HE-20 in the Excel file.

This immediately explains why this comes **after** all Claim-level evaluation.

That is much clearer than two question cards under one large Claim list.

# 3. HE-19.1 — Claim recall

The current question:

> Were all central theses or hypotheses advanced by the source extracted as claims?

is fundamentally correct, but I would slightly improve it.

### Heading

**HE-19.1 · Claim recall**

### Question

> **Does the extracted Claim set capture all central theses or hypotheses that the Source advances as part of its contribution?**

The phrase **Claim set** is useful: we are no longer evaluating one Claim.

## Criterion definition

I would substantially simplify the current long definition.

### How to judge

> Assess the extracted Claims against the **full paper**, not only its abstract, introduction or conclusion.
>
> A central thesis or hypothesis is missing when the paper advances it as part of its contribution but no extracted Claim adequately represents it.
>
> Do not count as missing:
>
> * a restatement of an already extracted Claim;
> * headings or signposting;
> * facts about the paper itself;
> * mandatory boilerplate;
> * uncommented quotations from primary legal sources; or
> * graph metadata.
>
> Apply the same standard to doctrinal, theoretical, empirical and technical papers. The number of expected Claims depends on the paper; there is no target Claim count.

This preserves what the workbook actually requires while removing the very long schema quotation. 

### Response

**○ Yes ○ In part ○ No**

No `Unclear`.

A useful interpretation here:

* **Yes** — no central contribution-level Claim is missing.
* **In part** — the main contribution is represented, but one or more relevant central Claims are omitted.
* **No** — the extracted set materially fails to represent the paper's central contribution.

That makes the partial category especially meaningful for recall.

# 6. Source-level conceptual coverage

I would not call it **HE-20.S** unless the team has explicitly chosen that identifier. That naming looks like an implementation invention.

Because this criterion arose during the 7 September calibration and has no direct row in the 5 September workbook, I would use either:

**HE-20S · Source conceptual coverage**

if you want it formally under the HE-20 family,

or more conservatively for now:

**Source-level conceptual coverage — added during calibration**

Until you freeze the final identifier.

The substance, however, should remain.

## Question

The current wording:

> Across all claims extracted from this paper, are the mapping-relevant concepts addressed by the source adequately represented?

is close.

I would use:

> **Taken across all extracted Claims, does the Concept mapping adequately represent the concepts that this Source materially engages for systematic mapping?**

The phrase **taken across all extracted Claims** is important.

## Criterion definition

I would rewrite the current definition considerably.

### How to judge

> Assess conceptual coverage at the **Source level**, after reviewing all Claims.
>
> The question is whether the Concept mappings, taken together, preserve the dimensions of the paper that matter for the systematic mapping review.
>
> A Concept does not need to appear on every individual Claim that could plausibly be related to it. Do not penalise the Brain merely because another conceptual association is possible.
>
> Mark a deficiency when a concept materially engaged by the paper is absent from the Source's mappings altogether, or is represented so incompletely that the paper would be mischaracterised in the resulting map.
>
> This is different from Claim-level Concept recall: an omission on one Claim may be harmless at Source level when the same mapping-relevant concept is adequately represented elsewhere in the paper.

This is almost exactly the problem the team worked through in the meeting: the relevant standard is **completeness for the mapping purpose**, not maximal conceptual linkage.

The generation rules also confirm that Concept families drive the two-dimensional maps of the field, which is why completeness at this level matters.

# 7. Avoid double-counting Claim recall and conceptual coverage

The UI should explicitly explain the distinction because otherwise reviewers may score the same omission twice.

I would put a small neutral note between the two sections:

> **Claim recall and conceptual coverage test different failures.**
> Claim recall asks whether a central proposition is missing. Conceptual coverage asks whether the extracted and mapped Claims collectively represent the paper's mapping-relevant concepts.

Example:

* Paper contains an important argument about **access to justice**, but no Claim captures that argument → **Claim recall problem** and likely also a downstream conceptual-coverage problem.
* The relevant Claim exists, but it is never mapped to the needed **access-to-justice** Concept → **Conceptual coverage problem**, not Claim recall.
* One Claim lacks a Concept but another Claim in the same paper already captures that conceptual dimension adequately → possibly a **Claim-level HE-20 issue**, but not necessarily a Source-level coverage failure.

That distinction will materially improve inter-rater agreement.

# 10. Bottom dynamics

Since this is the last substantive evaluation section:

**← CITES**  **Continue to Review →**

`Continue to Review` should require:

* HE-19.1 answered;
* HE-19.3 completed when required;
* source-level conceptual coverage answered;
* missing Concept field completed when required.

Then the evaluator reaches the **Review** page, which should not introduce new substantive judgments—it should identify incomplete responses across Source, Claims, Datasets, CITES and this page before the paper is marked complete.

So my main conclusions are:

* **Keep Claim recall.**
* **Restore HE-19.3 as its own free-text field; it is currently missing.**
* **Keep the new source-level conceptual-coverage idea because the 7 September meeting explicitly identified this missing level of evaluation.**
* But make clear that it is a **calibration-derived addition not present in the 5 September workbook**.
* Most importantly, **show the Source's aggregated Concept mappings before asking the evaluator to judge conceptual coverage**. The current UI asks a question without showing the evidence needed to answer it.
