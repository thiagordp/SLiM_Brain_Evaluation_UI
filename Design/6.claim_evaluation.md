# A. Claim

The Excel currently has four criteria here. 

### HE-03 · Claim definition

**Question**

> **Does this extracted item qualify as a single claim under the Brain's claim definition?**

**Criterion definition**

> A Claim is a central thesis or hypothesis that the paper advances as part of its contribution. It must express one contention that stands or falls together.
>
> The test is semantic, not grammatical. Parts should be separated when a reader could accept one and reject the other, when they require different Claim types or jurisdictions, or when one could be challenged without challenging the other. A multi-part thesis may remain one Claim when its parts jointly form one contention.
>
> Sentence count does not determine Claim boundaries.

This is clearer than exposing the schema prose almost verbatim while preserving the same rule. 

---

### HE-25 · No duplicate Claim

This is one place where the meeting clearly changed the wording.

Current Excel wording talks about a “restatement”. The meeting found that confusing because repetition **inside the paper itself is not the defect**. The defect is extracting the same contention twice into the Brain.

**Question**

> **Is this Claim distinct from every other Claim extracted from this paper?**

**Criterion definition**

> Evaluate the extracted Claim set, not repetition in the original paper. Two Claims are duplicates when the Brain has created two Claim records for the same contention from this paper.
>
> Different wording alone does not make two Claims distinct. If the same proposition has been extracted more than once, the answer is No.

If `In part` or `No`, the comment should explicitly ask:

> Identify the other Claim ID where possible.

And beside the question:

**Compare claims from this paper**

This opens a centred modal listing all Claim IDs and statements from the same Source.

That directly implements what the meeting clarified.

---

### HE-04 · Independent intelligibility

**Question**

> **Can this Claim be understood on its own, without missing context from the paper?**

**Criterion definition**

> The statement should contain enough information to be intelligible independently of its original paragraph. References such as “this work”, “this approach”, “the authors”, or unresolved pronouns should be replaced by their intended referents where necessary.
>
> Additional wording may clarify the same proposition, but must not introduce another proposition.

---

### HE-05 · Modality preserved

**Question**

> **Does the Claim preserve the strength and modality of the source?**

**Criterion definition**

> The Claim should preserve how strongly and in what mode the source expresses the proposition. A possibility should remain a possibility; a recommendation should remain a recommendation; a reported finding should remain a finding.
>
> For example, “may” must not become “will”, “suggests” must not become “shows”, and “should” must not become “must”.

This is the same substantive rule as the Excel, but easier for evaluators to apply.

## HE-06 · Textual grounding

I would **stop using “warrant the Claim” as the primary wording**.

During the meeting, the group initially treated anchors almost as if they had to reconstruct the argument supporting the Claim. The eventual clarification was different: anchors primarily establish that the Claim is genuinely grounded in the source rather than hallucinated. They are not expected to reproduce the entire chain of reasoning.

**Question**

> **Do the anchors provide sufficient textual grounding for this Claim in the source?**

**Criterion definition**

> Anchors are verbatim passages that show where the Claim is grounded in the source text. Their purpose is to establish that the Claim is stated or defensibly represented in the paper.
>
> They do **not** need to reproduce every argument or reason supporting the Claim.
>
> Taken together, they should cover the substantive content of the Claim sufficiently to make its attribution to the source defensible. They must not combine unrelated passages into a proposition the source does not make.
>
> Each anchor should have a valid source location.

This is the clearest meeting-driven revision on the Claim page. The Excel's older wording still describes anchors as “warranting” the proposition.

## HE-07 · Premise

The meeting then separated the premise from the anchors.

**Question**

> **Is the premise a defensible reconstruction of the ground the source gives for this Claim?**

I would explicitly use **defensible** because the meeting repeatedly converged on that standard.

**Criterion definition**

> The premise states, in one sentence, the reason or ground that the source gives for the Claim.
>
> Unlike an anchor, the premise is a structured reconstruction rather than a verbatim quotation. It should accurately represent the source's reasoning without adding a ground that the source does not provide.
>
> The premise should be consistent with `basis`. If the source states no ground and `basis` is `none_stated`, the premise should be empty and this criterion is not applicable.
>
> Merely naming a case, provision or dataset is not sufficient when the source gives a more specific reason or result.

If `basis = none_stated` and premise is empty, show:

> **Not applicable — no premise is expected because the source states no ground.**

Do not make the evaluator manually choose N/A.

## HE-08.2 · Positive form

The meeting spent considerable time clarifying applicability here.

**Question**

> **If this Claim describes legal practice, is `positive_form` correctly assigned? Otherwise, is it correctly marked `not_applicable`?**

**Criterion definition**

> `positive_form` applies only when a Claim describes what courts, legislators or regulators do.
>
> For such Claims:
>
> `general_rule` — a general pattern of decision or practice
> `trend` — change over time
> `split` — disagreement between courts or jurisdictions
> `frequency` — how often something occurs
> `existence` — at least one relevant instance exists
>
> For other Claims, the correct value is `not_applicable`.
>
> Evaluate what **the source represents**, not whether the described legal practice is objectively true.

That last sentence addresses the specific confusion raised in the meeting.

---

## HE-08.3 · Basis

The meeting decided that **“dominant” should be removed from the evaluator's task**, because several defensible ground classifications may exist.

**Question**

> **Is `basis` a defensible classification of the ground the source gives for this Claim, and is `basis_qualifier` correct where applicable?**

**Criterion definition**

> `basis` classifies the kind of ground the source offers:
>
> `case_law` · `legislation` · `dataset_or_experiment` · `argument` · `literature` · `none_stated`
>
> The evaluator should ask whether the recorded category is a defensible description of the ground used by the source. Do not require a uniquely “dominant” ground where more than one defensible reading exists.
>
> `basis_qualifier` applies only when `basis = case_law` and records whether the source treats the relevant proposition as a `holding`, `dictum`, or `unclear`.

This is a direct change from the older workbook wording, which still says “dominant kind of ground.” 

---

## HE-08.4 · Claim jurisdiction

**Question**

> **Do the jurisdiction fields correctly represent the legal scope of this Claim and how that scope was determined?**

**Criterion definition**

> `claim_jurisdiction` concerns the Claim itself and must not simply be copied from the Source's jurisdiction.
>
> `general` means the Claim is not tied to a particular legal system.
> `undetermined` means the source and its context do not permit the jurisdiction to be determined.
> `geographical_proxy:<code>` means the Claim itself is general but its supporting materials come entirely from the named jurisdiction.
>
> Where more than one jurisdiction is recorded, `jurisdiction_relation` explains their relationship. `jurisdiction_inferred` is true when the jurisdiction was derived from context rather than stated directly.
>
> Evaluate all of these fields together.

The current Excel explicitly treats these three pieces as one HE-08.4 judgment. 

---

## HE-08.5 · Temporal reference

**Question**

> **Does `temporal_reference` correctly represent the time or period described by this Claim?**

**Criterion definition**

> Use a specific date or period when the source explicitly situates the legal or factual state in time. Otherwise the correct value is `as_of_publication`.
>
> The Claim should record what the source said at the time of publication and must not be updated retrospectively using later developments.

This can stay very close to the workbook.

## HE-12 · Concept correctness

The meeting correctly identified that **correctness and completeness are two separate things**.

HE-12 should evaluate **precision only**.

**Question**

> **Is every Concept currently mapped to this Claim a defensible mapping?**

**Criterion definition**

> Evaluate the mappings that are already present. Each mapped Concept should represent a notion that this Claim substantively concerns.
>
> An existing anchor should be used where it captures the Claim without distortion. A non-anchor Concept is not automatically wrong: when both an anchor and another Concept apply, both may be mapped.
>
> Do not use this criterion to judge whether a relevant Concept is missing; that is evaluated separately under Concept completeness.

This separation is important because the workbook's older HE-12 definition partially mixes correctness with anchor selection. 

If three concepts are mapped and only two are correct:

**In part**

and the evaluator identifies the problematic Concept(s) in the comment.

## HE-21 · Candidate concept necessary

Only show this as an active question when the system knows that a candidate Concept was actually created for this Claim.

**Question**

> **Was creating this candidate Concept necessary because no existing anchor or equivalent Concept captured the Claim without distortion?**

**Criterion definition**

> A candidate Concept should be created only when the Claim cannot be adequately represented by the existing anchor set and no equivalent Concept already exists under another name.
>
> A new Concept should therefore add a genuinely needed notion rather than duplicate or rename an existing one.

If no candidate was created for this Claim:

> **Not applicable — no candidate Concept was created for this Claim.**

No user action required. The workbook already makes this criterion N/A in that situation.

## HE-20 · Concept completeness

This needs careful wording after the meeting.

The important point is **not** to require every concept that could theoretically be associated with the Claim. The test should be driven by the mapping-review purpose.

**Question**

> **Are all Concepts needed to represent what this Claim substantively concerns for mapping purposes included in its mapping?**

**Criterion definition**

> Evaluate whether a substantively relevant mapping dimension has been omitted.
>
> The Claim should be mapped to the concepts it genuinely engages in the relevant families: legal task, technique class and normative concern, plus another family where required.
>
> Do not penalise the Brain merely because an additional Concept could be associated with the Claim in the abstract. A missing Concept matters where its omission would leave out information that is relevant to mapping what the paper is about.

This reflects the late-meeting distinction between **conceptual possibility** and **completeness for systematic mapping**.

## Dynamics of pages between claims and after last claim

I would make the claim-to-claim dynamics very simple.

The workbook treats each Claim as its own evaluation unit—the old manual procedure was literally one duplicated Claim sheet per Claim—so the UI should preserve that separation.  At the same time, the meeting decisions we have been following imply that evaluators must be able to go back and revise earlier judgments before the paper is finally completed.

### Between claims



At the bottom of every Claim page:

**← Previous claim**  `Claim 4 of 14`  **Next claim →**

Behaviour:

* **Previous claim**: always available except on Claim 1.
* **Next claim**: moves to the next Claim and starts at the top.
* Answers autosave; moving between Claims never requires a separate Save button.
* The evaluator may return to any earlier Claim and revise it before the paper is marked complete.
* If the current Claim has missing required answers/comments, `Next claim` should still preferably **block and identify what is missing**. This keeps each Claim as a complete evaluation unit rather than scattering incomplete answers across the paper.
* Any open wiki/modal closes when moving to another Claim.

At the top, keep a compact indicator such as:

> **Claim 4 of 14 · Complete**

or:

> **Claim 4 of 14 · 12/15 items complete**

### After the last claim

On the final Claim, replace **Next claim →** with:

**Continue to Datasets →**

So:

```text
Claim 13
→ Next claim

Claim 14
→ Continue to Datasets
```

If the last Claim is incomplete, this button remains blocked in the same way.

After all Claims are complete, the main paper navigation should show:

> **Claims 14/14 ✓**

Then continue with the sequence we already fixed:

**Datasets → CITES → Claim recall → Review**

This is also consistent with the workbook structure: Claim-level evaluation is separate from Dataset evaluation, while Claim recall is a later **Source-level** question asking whether any central theses were omitted from the complete extracted Claim set. 

One detail I would add: even after reaching Datasets, **Claims remains clickable as a completed section**, so the evaluator can go back and amend Claim 7, Claim 12, etc., until the paper itself is eventually marked complete/finally submitted.
