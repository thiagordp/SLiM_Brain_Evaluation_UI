The more complete screenshot makes the remaining issues much clearer. I would revise this page substantially, but keep its basic structure.

I re-checked the 7 September meeting against the current Excel form. Three decisions from the meeting should override the older workbook mechanics on this page:

* **`order of dominance` should not be evaluated** for `contribution_type`; the team considered it too subjective and moved toward correctness, completeness and pertinence.
* The response scale should be **Yes / In part / No**, consistently across the human questions.
* A comment should be **required for In part and No**, so that the error can later be analysed.
* `other_versions`, although still present as HE-08.3 in the 5 September workbook, was discussed on 7 September as something already dealt with during processing and probably not worth a human question. I would therefore keep it as contextual metadata but **not score it here**. The older workbook still contains the pre-meeting version of these rules.  

The meeting transcript is here for reference: [Meeting 07-09](sandbox:/mnt/data/Meeting-07-09-Brain.srt).

# 5. Response scale

I would add one short line before the questions and then stop repeating the rules inside every card:

> **Response scale:** **Yes** = fully complies · **In part** = partly complies · **No** = does not comply.
> A comment is required for **In part** and **No**.

The 7 September discussion explicitly moved to this three-level verbal scale because a binary answer would force partially correct records into “No”, losing useful information for both agreement and error analysis. [Meeting 07-09](sandbox:/mnt/data/Meeting-07-09-Brain.srt)

Therefore I would **remove the current `Unclear` checkbox entirely** on this page. “Yes / In part / No” was subsequently agreed as the common human-evaluation scale.

# 6. HE-08.1 — Contribution type

This is the biggest textual change.

### Card heading

**HE-08.1 · Contribution type**

Do not repeat `contribution_type` as a technical subtitle unless useful.

### Question

Current:

> Are the selected contribution_type values correct, complete, and pertinent to the publication?

I would use:

> **Do the selected `contribution_type` values correctly and completely represent the publication's contribution, including its most pertinent type or types?**

This preserves the meeting's three concerns but explicitly removes the need to rank the complete list.

## Criterion definition

The current definition is too dense and partly contradicts the meeting because it still carries the old schema's “order of dominance” language.

I would rewrite the expandable panel as:

### Criterion definition

**How to judge**

Assess whether:

* every selected contribution type applies to the publication;
* no applicable contribution type is missing; and
* the selected values include the publication's most pertinent contribution type or types.

**Do not evaluate the order of the selected values.**

**Contribution types**

* **doctrinal** — describes, systematises or interprets existing law (*lex lata*);
* **normative** — argues what law, policy or practice should be, including reform or ethical recommendations;
* **theoretical** — develops or critiques concepts, taxonomies or frameworks without primarily reporting empirical results;
* **empirical_quantitative** — reports measurements, experiments, benchmarks, statistics or other numerical results;
* **empirical_qualitative** — reports interviews, case studies, qualitative content analysis or observation;
* **technical** — proposes or evaluates a system, model, dataset or method;
* **survey** — reviews existing literature as its main contribution.

The last definition matters because the meeting actually tested it on W0085: the group initially disagreed over whether something without an implementation could be “technical”, then returned to the definition and concluded that **proposing a method is enough**. So I would optionally add beneath `technical`:

> A publication may therefore be technical even when it proposes a method without implementing it.

That is a useful calibration clarification from the meeting, not a new criterion.

The underlying workbook confirms the same closed list and definitions, although it still contains the older dominance requirement. 

### Answer controls

Use one radio row:

**○ Yes ○ In part ○ No**

Remove `Unclear`.

### Comment

Current:

> Comment / evidence (optional)

This is now wrong.

Use:

**Comment / evidence**
*Required for In part or No.*

Placeholder:

> **Explain what is incorrect or missing. Where possible, give the corrected classification and the relevant page or section of the source.**

I would keep the text field available even after `Yes`, because an evaluator may still want to leave a useful note; it simply is not required in that case.

# 7. HE-08.2 — Source jurisdiction

### Card heading

**HE-08.2 · Source jurisdiction**

### Question

The current question is already good. I would make only a small stylistic change:

> **Does `source_jurisdiction` correctly represent the legal system or systems in which the publication is situated?**

## Criterion definition

I would rewrite the current dense text as:

### Criterion definition

**How to judge**

`source_jurisdiction` describes the legal setting of the **publication as a whole**. It is different from `claim_jurisdiction`, which is evaluated later for individual claims.

Assess whether the recorded value correctly reflects the legal system or systems in which the publication is situated.

* **general** — the publication is genuinely not situated in any particular legal system;
* **undetermined** — the publication does not provide enough information to determine a legal system.

These represent different situations and should not be treated as interchangeable.

When a specific jurisdiction is used:

* use the relevant country code;
* use `EU` for the European Union, `CoE` for the Council of Europe system and `INT` for public international law;
* use a sub-national unit only where the publication itself warrants that level of specificity.

Do not assign a jurisdiction merely because the paper discusses an isolated case, statute or example from that jurisdiction.

That last point is important for the kind of issue discussed during the meeting: a paper can use US cases as illustrations while still being situated at a general level.

The older Excel wording defines `source_jurisdiction` as the legal system in which the work is situated and explicitly distinguishes it from `claim_jurisdiction`.  The 7 September discussion then focused on making the difference between a genuinely general source and an indeterminate one clearer. [Meeting 07-09](sandbox:/mnt/data/Meeting-07-09-Brain.srt)

### Answer

Again:

**○ Yes ○ In part ○ No**

### Comment

Exactly the same component as HE-08.1:

**Comment / evidence**
*Required for In part or No.*

Placeholder:

> **Explain what is incorrect or missing. Where possible, give the corrected jurisdiction and the relevant page or section of the source.**

I would make the placeholder criterion-specific rather than use the exact same generic sentence everywhere.

# 8. Card formatting

The current cards are too tall largely because the full criterion definition occupies a lot of vertical space.

Default state should therefore be **collapsed**:

```text
HE-08.1 · Contribution type

Do the selected contribution_type values correctly and completely
represent the publication's contribution, including its most
pertinent type or types?

▸ Criterion definition

○ Yes     ○ In part     ○ No

Comment / evidence — Required for In part or No
[                                                   ]
```

When opened, the definition expands.

Visually:

* criterion ID: small muted text or part of the heading;
* question: normal body size, perhaps slightly heavier;
* definition expander: neutral grey background;
* answer row: more vertical space above/below;
* comments: textarea about 2–3 lines initially, expandable;
* one card per criterion.

The current use of green monospace for every field name/value is excessive. Reserve monospace for literal schema values such as `technical`, `CA-QC`, `general`, etc. Ordinary prose should remain ordinary text.
