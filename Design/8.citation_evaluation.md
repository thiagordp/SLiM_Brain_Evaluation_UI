The basic structure is right, but this page needs one important clarification at the top: **CITES does not mean “all references in the paper.”**

I checked the workbook again. HE-18 has exactly three elements: **HE-18.1 accuracy**, **HE-18.2 completeness**, and **HE-18.3 missing/spurious edges**. A CITES edge exists only when the current Source cites another Source that is already represented in the Brain; citations to works outside the Brain and citations to primary legal sources do not become CITES edges.  The 7 September meeting did not materially redefine the CITES criteria, so I would keep that substance and apply the general meeting decisions on wording and response mechanics. [Meeting 07-09](sandbox:/mnt/data/Meeting-07-09-Brain.srt)

## 1. Page title and explanation

I would replace:

**CITES edges**

with:

## Citations within the Brain

Then a short explanation:

> **This section evaluates citations between this paper and other papers represented in the Brain. It does not reproduce the paper's full reference list. A citation becomes a CITES edge only when the cited publication is also a Source in the Brain.**

Then one additional line:

> **References to publications outside the Brain, and citations to legislation, case law or other primary legal sources, are intentionally excluded.**

That directly prevents the misunderstanding you identified: a paper may contain ten or fifty references while only one CITES edge appears.

I would say **Brain**, not “evaluation set”, because the graph was built over the full 50-source Brain. A citation can therefore be relevant even if the cited paper is not one of the papers assigned to this particular evaluator.

# 4. Add a Source registry tool

This is particularly important for **completeness**.

The evaluator has read the paper and can recognise its bibliography, but cannot reasonably be expected to remember which cited publications are among the 50 Sources in the Brain.

So beside the outgoing-citation section, add:

**Browse Brain Sources**

This opens a centred searchable modal containing the Source registry:

* Source ID;
* W-ID;
* title;
* authors;
* year.

Search by title/author/ID.

This is not another evaluation task. It is simply the context needed to answer HE-18.2 reliably.

Without something like this, HE-18.2 is unnecessarily difficult.

# 5. HE-18.1 — CITES accuracy

### Visible heading

**HE-18.1 · CITES accuracy**

### Question

I would rewrite the current sentence as:

> **Do all outgoing CITES edges shown above correspond to citations that this paper actually makes to the indicated Brain Sources, with the correct direction from this paper to the cited Source?**

This is simpler while preserving the exact test.

### Criterion definition

**How to judge**

> A CITES edge records that one Source cites another Source represented in the Brain.
>
> For every outgoing edge shown above, verify that:
>
> * the current paper actually cites the indicated publication;
> * the cited publication has been matched to the correct Brain Source; and
> * the edge runs from the citing Source to the cited Source.
>
> Do not evaluate whether the citation is approving, critical, important or substantively correct. CITES records the **existence of the citation**, not its argumentative role.
>
> Citations to publications outside the Brain and citations to primary legal sources do not belong here.

That last distinction is already part of the workbook's definition.

# 6. HE-18.2 — CITES completeness

### Heading

**HE-18.2 · CITES completeness**

### Question

> **Are all citations made by this paper to other Sources represented in the Brain captured by outgoing CITES edges?**

This is clearer than:

> Is every citation from this source to another source already in the corpus represented by a CITES edge?

### Criterion definition

> Compare the paper's citations with the Sources represented in the Brain.
>
> Every citation from this paper to another publication that is already a Brain Source should have a CITES edge.
>
> A missing edge is a defect when:
>
> 1. this paper cites another publication; and
> 2. that publication is represented as a Source in the Brain; but
> 3. no outgoing CITES edge is present.
>
> Do not count as missing:
>
> * citations to publications outside the Brain;
> * legislation;
> * case law;
> * regulatory guidance; or
> * other primary legal materials.

This is exactly the corpus-boundary logic in the workbook. 

Right beside the criterion definition or question:

**Browse Brain Sources**

### Response

**○ Yes ○ In part ○ No**

### Comment

**Comment / evidence**
*Required for In part or No.*

Placeholder:

> Briefly explain which citation or citations appear to be missing.

# 7. HE-18.3 is currently missing from the UI

This is the main substantive omission in the screenshot.

The workbook explicitly contains:

**HE-18.3 · Missing / spurious edges**

and asks reviewers to list the specific problems whenever the preceding judgments fail. 

I would add it after HE-18.2.

### HE-18.3 · Missing or spurious CITES edges

Show this field when either HE-18.1 or HE-18.2 is **In part** or **No**.

> **List the CITES edges that are missing or spurious.**

Instruction:

> For a **missing** edge, identify the cited Source ID or publication.
> For a **spurious** edge, identify the incorrectly linked cited Source.

Textarea placeholder:

> Missing: SRC-xxxx — [title]
> Spurious: SRC-yyyy — [title]

One small correction to the workbook is needed here. It currently says to identify a spurious “edge id”, but the same workbook defines CITES records as `{type, from, to, run_id}`—there is no separate CITES edge ID.  Therefore the **target Source ID** is the stable identifier the evaluator should use.

If both HE-18.1 and HE-18.2 are Yes, simply show:

> **No missing or spurious CITES edges identified.**

and no textarea is needed.

# 8. Important zero-edge case

The interface must handle a Source with **no outgoing CITES edges** carefully.

Show:

> **The Brain generated no outgoing CITES edges for this Source.**

Then:

* **HE-18.1 accuracy:** automatically **Not applicable — there are no generated edges to assess.**
* **HE-18.2 completeness:** still must be evaluated.

This matters because:

> zero generated edges ≠ automatically correct.

The Brain could have missed a citation to another Brain Source.

If HE-18.2 is In part/No, HE-18.3 opens so the missing citation can be listed.

Incoming citations remain context only regardless.
