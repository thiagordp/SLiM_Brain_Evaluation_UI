# DST-0023 — ContractNLI

External benchmark for document-level natural language inference over contracts, introduced by Koreeda and Manning (2021) and used by SRC-0022, which describes it as one of the few benchmarks grounded in authentic contract language whose original labels, produced by legally trained annotators, largely reflect legal interpretation [CLM-0022-004]. The paper does not state the jurisdiction of the contract texts; their label vocabulary and hypotheses are in English.

## Papers and claims resting on it

- SRC-0022 re-annotates its examples under a strict formal entailment definition, finding substantial label shifts, primarily ENTAILMENT to NEUTRAL [CLM-0022-004], which it holds are not errors but assumption-dependent cases [CLM-0022-005]; it constructs minimal pairs over the divergent cases [CLM-0022-006] and evaluates three paradigms across five LLMs on it, finding that formal structure improves accuracy but accuracy does not imply faithful reasoning [CLM-0022-007], that the SMT pipeline is more conservative [CLM-0022-008], that three failure modes recur [CLM-0022-009], and that NEUTRAL to ENTAILMENT misclassification dominates [CLM-0022-010].

## Annotation

Original labels by legally trained annotators, largely reflecting legal interpretation; re-annotation by the authors of SRC-0022 under a strict formal definition of entailment [CLM-0022-004]. No inter-annotator agreement is reported by SRC-0022.
