# DST-0025 — dataset of LLM explanations annotated by two students trained in influencer marketing law

## Papers and claims

SRC-0023 introduces this dataset and uses it for its explanation-quality findings: that legal input in the prompt improves perceived explanation quality without consistently improving detection accuracy [CLM-0023-005], the inference that LLMs rely on internal heuristics rather than reading and applying legal norms [CLM-0023-006], the distribution of error types dominated by missing and unclear citations with rare hallucinations [CLM-0023-007], the highest mistaken-cue rate on undisclosed ads [CLM-0023-008], and the concluding prescription to pair performance metrics with legal-reasoning audits [CLM-0023-013].

## Language and jurisdiction

The annotated explanations concern English-language Instagram posts assessed under Dutch advertising self-regulation, so the jurisdiction is the Netherlands [CLM-0023-005] [CLM-0023-012].

## Annotation

Two research assistants with legal knowledge, trained by domain experts, annotated the presence of seven error types in each explanation and rated helpfulness on a 1-5 scale; on 10 overlapping posts they achieved 89.29% absolute agreement and 0.37 Krippendorff's Alpha [CLM-0023-007].
