# DST-0020 — 30 recent ECtHR Article 10 cases

Introduced and released by SRC-0020 to assess LLM capabilities in legal judgment forecasting and reasoning [CLM-0020-006]. Its texts are English-language judgments of the European Court of Human Rights; the ECtHR's supranational Council of Europe system has no code in the jurisdiction vocabulary, so the field records `undetermined` (logged). SRC-0020's experimental claims rest on it: the overall reasoning-quality finding [CLM-0020-001], the judge reliability-validity finding [CLM-0020-002], the prompting comparison [CLM-0020-003], the accuracy-reasoning decoupling [CLM-0020-004], the doctrinal-internalisation reading [CLM-0020-008], the step-wise results [CLM-0020-009], the factual-reference results [CLM-0020-010], the hidden-reasoning token results [CLM-0020-011] and the transparency concern drawn from them — that a closed model's disclosed assessment need not reflect its actual internal computation [CLM-0020-012], the recurring failure modes [CLM-0020-013], the Tergek v. Türkiye qualitative reading [CLM-0020-014], and the inter-annotator agreement results [CLM-0020-015]. Annotation: model responses were rated by three senior law students trained in ECtHR jurisprudence under expert supervision, on step occurrence, step comprehensiveness and overall conciseness, and by three LLM judges under the same protocol; agreement is reported — 97% exact on occurrence and 87-91% within one point on the 1-5 criteria, with low chance-corrected coefficients (κ/α = 0.09-0.14) that the paper reads as a prevalence/restricted-range artifact [CLM-0020-015].
