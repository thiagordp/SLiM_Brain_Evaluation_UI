# DST-0019 — DIAS Corpus

U.S. court opinions (English, U.S. jurisdiction) on whether police officers had reasonable suspicion of drug trafficking in drug interdiction stops, obtained through the Harvard Caselaw Access Project; the corpus originates outside this graph's sources, in the prior work the paper credits for its creation.

## Papers using it

- SRC-0018 (Using LLMs to Discover Legal Factors) works with a random subset of 173 of the 300 cases and rests on it the claims about LLM-based factor discovery and its evaluation [CLM-0018-001, CLM-0018-002, CLM-0018-003, CLM-0018-004, CLM-0018-005, CLM-0018-006, CLM-0018-007, CLM-0018-009].

## Annotation and agreement

As SRC-0018 reports, an attorney expert in drug interdiction law annotated a sub-sample of 103 of the 173 cases, identifying the paragraphs containing the court's analysis and conclusion and the gold-standard factor sets [CLM-0018-004]. Comparing the model-identified spans' factor sets with the expert's, the paper reports average accuracy 0.96, precision 0.99 and recall 0.97, and reads the high recall as showing the process is reliable [CLM-0018-004]. No inter-annotator agreement between multiple human annotators is reported for this corpus in SRC-0018.
