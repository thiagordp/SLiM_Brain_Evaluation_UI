# DST-0009 — Layperson

## Papers and claims

SRC-0011 uses this dataset, together with DST-0008, for its headline result that the SyLeR framework achieves the best performance across all metrics against legal-specific and open-domain baselines [CLM-0011-010], the attribution of legal LLMs' strength to embedded legal knowledge [CLM-0011-011], the finding that fine-tuning-based reasoning enhancement outperforms prompt-based methods [CLM-0011-012], the ablation of SyLeR's three components [CLM-0011-013], and the cross-domain generalization result [CLM-0011-014]. It uses this dataset alone for the backbone-robustness comparison with Llama3-8B-Instruct [CLM-0011-016] and for the human evaluation of correctness, logicality, explainability and trustworthiness on 50 randomly selected questions [CLM-0011-017].

## Language and jurisdiction

The texts are Chinese-language user questions and responses from the Hualv website, where non-legal professionals seek advice from lawyers and legal practitioners, so their jurisdiction is China (CN) [CLM-0011-010].

## Annotation and agreement

The responses come from lawyers and legal practitioners answering user questions on the Hualv website; the paper using it reports no further annotation of the dataset itself and no inter-annotator agreement [CLM-0011-010]. In the human evaluation conducted on 50 of its questions, three graduate students majoring in Chinese law scored anonymized model responses from 1 to 5 and scores were averaged; no inter-rater agreement is reported [CLM-0011-017].
