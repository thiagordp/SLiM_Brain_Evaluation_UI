# CPT-democratic-legitimacy

## What it means

The concern whether a method of legal decision-making derives adequate authority from democratic processes and the constitutional role of courts, rather than from crowdsourced opinion or the design choices of technocratic elites. A candidate concept coined during the ingest of SRC-0019 and drawn from its claims [CLM-0019-003] [CLM-0019-004] [CLM-0019-012].

## Claims

### Interpretative

**US**

- Substituting a polling proxy — a panel drawn from the public or a large language model prediction of aggregated public opinion — for a judge's individual reasoning is not the process mandated for legal decisions by the text of the US Constitution and by US legal culture; a judge who handed over her ultimate decision to such a proxy would be violating her duty to decide the case. — rests on legal sources (SRC-0019). [CLM-0019-003]
- Pushed too far, the very aspect of textualism that seems to promise greater democratic legitimacy — decision-making based on an empirical investigation of ordinary people's understanding of legal text — undermines the interpretive method's political legitimacy; the problem is general to all textualist interpretation but becomes more visible when large language models are used for textualist analysis. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0019). [CLM-0019-004]

**general**

- Large language models are not a democratic technology, at least as currently manufactured, and there is something decidedly undemocratic about allowing the engineering decisions of a few technocratic elites to influence the operation of the courts. — rests on literature (SRC-0019). [CLM-0019-012]
- In legal judgment forecasting, appropriate legal reasoning prevails over predictive accuracy: a wrong prediction can arise from genuine ambiguity, narrative complexity, lack of context or subjectivity, whereas appropriate reasoning is the legally meaningful virtue that legitimises juridical bodies in democratic societies. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0020). [CLM-0020-016]
