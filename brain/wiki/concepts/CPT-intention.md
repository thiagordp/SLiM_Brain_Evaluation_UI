# CPT-intention

## What it means

The interpretive canon that looks to the intention of the law-maker or of the parties as the ground of a text's meaning. An anchor concept, given in advance in the grid of `schema/concept.md`; the corpus uses it as the claims below show [CLM-0010-002] [CLM-0010-003] [CLM-0010-004].

## Claims

### Descriptive

**US**

- Some of what lawyers, both academic and practicing, believe about contract law emanates from illusions and delusions embedded in and around the doctrinal canon — phrases like 'the meeting of the minds' or 'the document speaks for itself' — which are not easily dismissed as mere metaphors and persist because it is difficult to demarcate myth from history or belief from knowledge. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0025). [CLM-0025-001]

**general**

- In contemporary legal theory and philosophy, the possibility of an agent having a mental state called intention is often postulated as the condition of the possibility of law-creation: intention to legislate is constitutive of the law-creating act, in the sense that law cannot be created absent an intention on the part of the author. — rests on literature (SRC-0010). [CLM-0010-002]
- In legal theory, the existence of an intention behind the law is widely treated as the condition of the possibility of law-interpretation: on intentionalist accounts, interpretation is impossible without intention, and there is no meaning in the lack of an author or of an intended meaning. — rests on literature (SRC-0010). [CLM-0010-003]
- Large language model AI in general, and ChatGPT in particular, generate legal texts without having any intention: generative artificial intelligence models do not possess mental states, dispositions, or attitudes that constitute intentions, and there is no evidence of any internal life in large language models. — rests on literature (SRC-0010). [CLM-0010-008]
- Even theorists whose work justifies a text-focused approach to contract interpretation, such as Alan Schwartz and Robert Scott, and the proponents of LLM-based generative interpretation pay lip service to the 'mutual intention' or 'shared intention of the parties' trope even as their own theses undercut it. — rests on literature (SRC-0025). [CLM-0025-005]

### Interpretative

**US**

- 'Mutual intention' and its corresponding metaphor 'the meeting of the minds' are metaphysical nonsense: there is no such thing as a mutual intention of the parties in a colorable contract interpretation dispute, and no shared mind or 'shared intention' thought bubble that can be known rather than posited by belief. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0025). [CLM-0025-002]
- The third party who observes and understands the objective manifestations of contractual agreement can be a large language model, even though it is a proxy for a human mind and does not think like one; this belies the need for the shared-intention illusion, and since 'shared intention' can never be known, the result will be more satisfying as a rational result than a mere coin flip or roll of the dice. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0025). [CLM-0025-008]

**general**

- At least three kinds of intention are relevant to law-creation: type intention, the intention to produce an object type such as a rulebook regulating behaviour; token intention, the intention to produce a token of a type with determinate content; and causal intention, the intention that the enacted text cause a certain kind of behaviour. One kind of intention does not necessarily entail the others. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0010). [CLM-0010-004]
- According to the dominant positions in legal theory and philosophy that make intention a condition of the possibility of law-creation and law-interpretation, the outputs of ChatGPT cannot be considered law: in the lack of anything that might reasonably be called an intention in ChatGPT, its textual outputs resembling legal texts cannot count as law, and a determination of the meaning of the outputs of large language models cannot be considered interpretation. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0010). [CLM-0010-011]
- When human intervention in the generation of a legal text is limited to formulating the prompt given to an AI, the human type intention and causal intention behind the prompt make the output an intentional creation, but the token intention supposed to be behind the linguistic content of the act is lacking, which eliminates the possibility of law-interpretation based on the actual token intention of the legislator or legal author. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0010). [CLM-0010-012]
- It is perfectly conceivable to have artefacts without any authorial intention behind them, as long as the use of the artefact attributes and maintains its proper function; within artefact theory, outputs generated by artificial intelligence can be considered legal artefacts as long as they are used as law so that their proper function is the legal regulation of human behaviour. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0010). [CLM-0010-013]
- The recipients of a text are fully equipped to determine the use functions of the text, confer the necessary legal authority on its maker or generator, and determine the normative content of the text; an intention, real or imagined, can play a role in this determination but is not necessary for the text to give rise to a determinate normative legal meaning. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0010). [CLM-0010-014]
- Outputs of large language models can count as law on the condition that their function is legal: the ability of agents without intentions, like ChatGPT, to create legal text is an argument in favour of the thesis that law can be created without intention behind the creation and that non-intentional creation can be interpreted to arrive at legal norms. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0010). [CLM-0010-015]

### Prescriptive

**US**

- Parties should accept randomness and uncertainty as part of their calculation when deciding whether to litigate or settle a contract dispute; one effect of the misguided focus on shared intention is that parties approach litigation with the idea of vindicating the right answer, rather than accepting that it is a crapshoot based on somebody's reconstruction of the parties' objective manifestations. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0025). [CLM-0025-011]

**general**

- The sooner the illusions of provable mutual intentions are discarded and the delusions that contracts are anything other than imperfect maps on a highly complex reality put aside, the better off contracting parties will be; it takes a village to interpret the signs people make to each other, because there are no private languages and the words and phrases are borrowed from the community in an attempt to be understood by way of objective manifestations. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0025). [CLM-0025-018]
