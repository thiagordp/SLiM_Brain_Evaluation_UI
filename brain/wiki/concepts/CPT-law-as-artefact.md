# CPT-law-as-artefact

## What it means

The view that law is an artefact — an object brought into being by agents, classically understood as intentionally created for a purpose — and the debate over whether authorial intention or instead use and function determines its artefactual character. A candidate concept coined during the ingest of SRC-0010 and drawn from its claims [CLM-0010-013] [CLM-0010-014] [CLM-0010-015].

## Claims

### Interpretative

**general**

- It is perfectly conceivable to have artefacts without any authorial intention behind them, as long as the use of the artefact attributes and maintains its proper function; within artefact theory, outputs generated by artificial intelligence can be considered legal artefacts as long as they are used as law so that their proper function is the legal regulation of human behaviour. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0010). [CLM-0010-013]
- The recipients of a text are fully equipped to determine the use functions of the text, confer the necessary legal authority on its maker or generator, and determine the normative content of the text; an intention, real or imagined, can play a role in this determination but is not necessary for the text to give rise to a determinate normative legal meaning. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0010). [CLM-0010-014]
- Outputs of large language models can count as law on the condition that their function is legal: the ability of agents without intentions, like ChatGPT, to create legal text is an argument in favour of the thesis that law can be created without intention behind the creation and that non-intentional creation can be interpreted to arrive at legal norms. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0010). [CLM-0010-015]
