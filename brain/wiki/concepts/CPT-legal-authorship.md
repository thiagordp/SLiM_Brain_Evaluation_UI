# CPT-legal-authorship

## What it means

The condition of being an author of law — the capacity of an agent to produce texts that count as law, traditionally tied to a determinate, normatively authorised and intentional author — and whether non-human agents can occupy that position. A candidate concept coined during the ingest of SRC-0010 and drawn from its claims [CLM-0010-001] [CLM-0010-002] [CLM-0010-011] [CLM-0010-012] [CLM-0010-015].

## Claims

### Descriptive

**general**

- In the philosophical debate on generative artificial intelligence and law, most attention has gone to the normative question whether AI should create and interpret law, while the prior question whether AI can create and interpret law — whether AI is capable of producing outputs that can be deemed law, and whether AI can be a legal author — has remained largely unattended. — rests on literature (SRC-0010). [CLM-0010-001]
- In contemporary legal theory and philosophy, the possibility of an agent having a mental state called intention is often postulated as the condition of the possibility of law-creation: intention to legislate is constitutive of the law-creating act, in the sense that law cannot be created absent an intention on the part of the author. — rests on literature (SRC-0010). [CLM-0010-002]

### Interpretative

**general**

- According to the dominant positions in legal theory and philosophy that make intention a condition of the possibility of law-creation and law-interpretation, the outputs of ChatGPT cannot be considered law: in the lack of anything that might reasonably be called an intention in ChatGPT, its textual outputs resembling legal texts cannot count as law, and a determination of the meaning of the outputs of large language models cannot be considered interpretation. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0010). [CLM-0010-011]
- When human intervention in the generation of a legal text is limited to formulating the prompt given to an AI, the human type intention and causal intention behind the prompt make the output an intentional creation, but the token intention supposed to be behind the linguistic content of the act is lacking, which eliminates the possibility of law-interpretation based on the actual token intention of the legislator or legal author. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0010). [CLM-0010-012]
- Outputs of large language models can count as law on the condition that their function is legal: the ability of agents without intentions, like ChatGPT, to create legal text is an argument in favour of the thesis that law can be created without intention behind the creation and that non-intentional creation can be interpreted to arrive at legal norms. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0010). [CLM-0010-015]
