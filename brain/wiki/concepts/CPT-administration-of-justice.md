# CPT-administration-of-justice

## What it means

The orderly functioning of courts and judicial processes, including the burdens and disruptions that litigation phenomena and technologies impose on judicial officers, court staff and public resources. A candidate concept coined during the ingest of SRC-0006 and drawn from its claims [CLM-0006-004] [CLM-0006-005] [CLM-0006-006] [CLM-0006-012] [CLM-0006-018] [CLM-0006-020].

## Claims

### Descriptive

**AU**

- Pseudolaw arguments are without any legal merit and do not work; courts have rejected them as 'obvious nonsense', 'pseudo-legal gibberish' and an unnecessary waste of scarce public and judicial resources. — rests on legal sources (SRC-0006). [CLM-0006-004]
- Pseudolaw arguments have proliferated, with significant growth in the years since the COVID-19 pandemic; pseudolaw matters now occur on a daily basis in Australian courts and in some jurisdictions are already seen to profoundly reshape litigation. — rests on literature (SRC-0006). [CLM-0006-005]
- Pseudolaw is particularly disruptive to courts: each pseudolaw case has a disproportionate impact compared to other cases, and the burden of dealing with pseudolaw matters is having a profound impact on the courts. — rests on literature (SRC-0006). [CLM-0006-006]
- Pseudolaw is beginning to interact with the widespread use of generative AI, particularly large language models, in a particularly alarming manner: a litigant in person has used an LLM to generate real-time responses to questions from the bench while advancing pseudolaw-like arguments, and instances of written submissions containing clear hallucinations are becoming common. — rests on factual basis (SRC-0006). [CLM-0006-012]

### Prescriptive

**general**

- In responding to pseudolaw, both mockery and engagement should be avoided — pragmatic disinterest in the argument, but not the person, is the preferable approach; responses should vary depending on the type of user confronted (the naive, the true believer, the mercenary and the guru); and, because the form and performance of pseudolaw matters, the most successful responses are aimed at disrupting the performance. — rests on literature (SRC-0006). [CLM-0006-018]

### Predictive

**AU**

- If turbocharged through its interaction with generative AI, pseudolaw has the potential to become an existential threat: the widespread utilisation of technologies to create outputs that look legally meaningful yet are substantively nonsensical would risk fundamentally disrupting almost all aspects of legal processes. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0006). [CLM-0006-020]
