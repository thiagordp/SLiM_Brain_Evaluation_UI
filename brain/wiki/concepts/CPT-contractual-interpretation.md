# CPT-contractual-interpretation

## What it means

Interpretation whose object is a contract or a contract-like private instrument, such as a licence or a binding agreement. An anchor concept, given in advance in the grid of `schema/concept.md`; the corpus uses it as the claims below show [CLM-0001-005] [CLM-0007-002] [CLM-0007-004].

## Claims

### Descriptive

**EU**

- Data Processing Agreements — legally binding contracts formalizing the controller-processor relationship under GDPR Article 28 — vary considerably in structure and terminology in practice; relevant details such as role definitions or security measures are often distributed across paragraphs or depend on contextual interpretation, causing sentence-level analysis to frequently fall short. — rests on literature (SRC-0001). [CLM-0001-005]

**US**

- Some of what lawyers, both academic and practicing, believe about contract law emanates from illusions and delusions embedded in and around the doctrinal canon — phrases like 'the meeting of the minds' or 'the document speaks for itself' — which are not easily dismissed as mere metaphors and persist because it is difficult to demarcate myth from history or belief from knowledge. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0025). [CLM-0025-001]

**general**

- Publicly available dataset licenses frequently lack clarity and standardized formats regarding usage rights and obligations, particularly whether commercial use is permitted, posing significant legal risks and making accurate interpretation challenging even for software IP lawyers. — rests on literature (SRC-0012). [CLM-0012-001]
- Dataset licenses commonly used in publicly available datasets fall into three categories, each posing distinct interpretation challenges: General Licenses, often adapted from open-source software formats, present non-straightforward obligations when applied to datasets; Customized Licenses contain highly specific, context-dependent clauses that add complexity; and Official Terms of Use or Service are characterized by dense, legally nuanced language and complex technical jargon. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0012). [CLM-0012-002]
- Even theorists whose work justifies a text-focused approach to contract interpretation, such as Alan Schwartz and Robert Scott, and the proponents of LLM-based generative interpretation pay lip service to the 'mutual intention' or 'shared intention of the parties' trope even as their own theses undercut it. — rests on literature (SRC-0025). [CLM-0025-005]

**undetermined**

- A hybrid system that pairs a coverage-aware retriever with symbolic rule-based reasoning to surface relevant medical coverage policy language, organize it into explicit facts and rules, and generate auditable rationales minimizes the number of LLM inferences required, achieving a 44% reduction in inference cost alongside a 4.5% improvement in F1 score. — rests on factual basis (SRC-0007). [CLM-0007-002]
- Standard semantic search is ill-suited for retrieving the policy language that governs a procedure code's coverage, because the signals that determine benefit status are often orthogonal to thematic content: passages thematically close to a procedure often do not determine its benefit status, while the actual coverage clause is often a concise exclusion or limitation found in a separate section. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0007). [CLM-0007-004]
- Re-annotating ContractNLI examples under a strict formal definition of entailment yields a substantial proportion of label shifts, primarily from ENTAILMENT to NEUTRAL, revealing a systematic gap between pragmatic legal interpretation and strict formal entailment. — rests on factual basis (SRC-0022). [CLM-0022-004]

### Interpretative

**US**

- 'Mutual intention' and its corresponding metaphor 'the meeting of the minds' are metaphysical nonsense: there is no such thing as a mutual intention of the parties in a colorable contract interpretation dispute, and no shared mind or 'shared intention' thought bubble that can be known rather than posited by belief. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0025). [CLM-0025-002]
- There is no 'correct answer' at which the exercise of contract interpretation should aim; parties who submit an interpretation dispute to a third party rather than settling have gone to war in a form of sublimated violence, the result of which is as likely a crapshoot as anything else. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0025). [CLM-0025-003]
- Meaning in law, as distinguished from meaning in fact, is a disembodied meaning derived from the community; LLM-based generative interpretation of contracts is just that, derived digitally rather than through the analog musings of a judge or jury, and the data it operates on is community meaning on a vastly larger scale — millions or billions of instances of community word usage rather than the particular usages of the contract parties. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0025). [CLM-0025-007]
- The third party who observes and understands the objective manifestations of contractual agreement can be a large language model, even though it is a proxy for a human mind and does not think like one; this belies the need for the shared-intention illusion, and since 'shared intention' can never be known, the result will be more satisfying as a rational result than a mere coin flip or roll of the dice. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0025). [CLM-0025-008]
- The academic text-versus-context debate in contract interpretation is a false dichotomy: a practicing lawyer argues as a textualist or a contextualist according to the client's interest in the dispute, and sensible courts reject binary absolutism, sometimes following the text and sometimes looking at the context as common sense dictates. — rests on legal sources (SRC-0025). [CLM-0025-009]

**general**

- Legal interpretation and formal logic are not different levels of rigor but different modes of reasoning: legal interpretation draws on background knowledge and contextual inference, while formal logic demands that all inferences be explicitly grounded in the text, so a legally sound conclusion may be formally invalid because the norm it relies on is nowhere stated in the contract. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0022). [CLM-0022-002]

**undetermined**

- The label shifts observed when contract entailment examples are re-annotated under a strict formal definition are not errors: they are cases where the original conclusion depends on background legal knowledge or contextual assumptions reasonable for a lawyer to invoke but absent from the text. — rests on factual basis (SRC-0022). [CLM-0022-005]

### Prescriptive

**US**

- Parties should accept randomness and uncertainty as part of their calculation when deciding whether to litigate or settle a contract dispute; one effect of the misguided focus on shared intention is that parties approach litigation with the idea of vindicating the right answer, rather than accepting that it is a crapshoot based on somebody's reconstruction of the parties' objective manifestations. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0025). [CLM-0025-011]

**general**

- The sooner the illusions of provable mutual intentions are discarded and the delusions that contracts are anything other than imperfect maps on a highly complex reality put aside, the better off contracting parties will be; it takes a village to interpret the signs people make to each other, because there are no private languages and the words and phrases are borrowed from the community in an attempt to be understood by way of objective manifestations. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0025). [CLM-0025-018]

### Predictive

**US**

- Large language models may be the adjudicatory technology that resolves the text-versus-context issue at the practical lawyering scale where it matters: as the technology improves, judges can relax old safeguards towards a more inclusive interpretive approach. — rests on literature (SRC-0025). [CLM-0025-010]
- As to whether the use of large language models in contract interpretation will make outcomes more or less certain and thereby reduce litigation by checking opportunism, the uncertainty may be so great that it does not make any difference, and trying to limit opportunism is a fool's game. — rests on abstract considerations (SRC-0025). [CLM-0025-012]

## Open questions

- Do the signals that govern legal relevance defeat similarity-based retrieval even when document structure is preserved?
- Is community word usage an empirical ground for legal meaning, or a value-laden construction in disguise?
- May an adjudicator delegate the observer's role in interpretation to a model without violating the duty to decide?
- Are interpretive determinations inherently normative tasks that models should not perform?
- Can LLMs resolve the choice of relevant context in interpretation, or only relocate it?
- Should model outputs ever outrank other evidence of meaning, such as dictionaries, in interpretation?
