# SRC-0017 — Leveraging LLM for Identification and Extraction of Normative Statements

## What the paper argues

The paper starts from the position that explicit traffic rules can be directly programmed into automated driving systems, while implicit traffic rules — which emerge from judicial decisions and may not be formally written in statutes — are crucial for autonomous vehicles to navigate ambiguous or unusual driving scenarios [CLM-0017-001], and that manual expert extraction of normative statements from court decisions, though highly accurate, is so time-consuming and labor-intensive that more efficient methods are urgently needed [CLM-0017-002]. Because a single straightforward prompt often leads a large language model to imprecise results [CLM-0017-003] and state-of-the-art multi-step prompting techniques remain largely restricted to simple tasks with high inference costs [CLM-0017-004], the authors propose Chain-of-Instructions (CoI) prompting, an advanced variant of Chain-of-Thought that replaces implicit reasoning directives with explicit stepwise instructions [CLM-0017-005]. In zero-shot extraction of normative sentences related to § 6 StVO from German court decisions with GPT-4o, CoI achieved superior Recall and F2 against Standard, Chain-of-Thought and Layer-of-Thoughts prompting, extracting 108 of 125 gold normative sentences [CLM-0017-006], while the complexity of legal language still kept GPT-4o from consistently extracting the most relevant statements [CLM-0017-007] and letting GPT-4o generate its own CoI prompts proved inconsistent and weaker [CLM-0017-008]. Layered decomposition of the task, with Standard prompts in early layers and CoI in the final one, further enhances effectiveness [CLM-0017-009]. Even at moderate precision, cutting the review workload from 6,242 sentences to 560 would significantly streamline manual review [CLM-0017-010]; the zero-shot setting makes the methodology efficient and reusable without fine-tuning [CLM-0017-011]; the approach constitutes a scalable framework that can continuously update an implicit rule corpus for autonomous vehicle pipelines as new decisions are issued [CLM-0017-012]; and F2 is preferred as the evaluation measure to reduce the risk of overlooking normative statements [CLM-0017-013].

## Claims

### Descriptive

- Explicit traffic rules such as speed limits and right-of-way regulations can be directly programmed into automated driving systems, but implicit traffic rules — which emerge from judicial decisions and may not be formally written in statutes — are crucial for autonomous vehicles to navigate ambiguous or unusual driving scenarios. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0017-001]
- Manual review of court decisions by legal experts to identify and extract normative statements related to specific traffic rules ensures high accuracy but is highly time-consuming and labor-intensive, and as the number of court decisions grows there is an urgent need for more efficient methods of extracting the implicit rules they contain. — general; rests on literature. [CLM-0017-002]
- Solving complex tasks with a single straightforward prompt often leads a large language model to imprecise or incorrect results, largely due to generative Transformer models' left-to-right, one-token-at-a-time architecture. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0017-003]
- State-of-the-art multi-step prompting techniques such as Chain-of-Thought, Tree of Thoughts and Graph of Thoughts are still largely restricted to relatively simple tasks such as simple math and reasoning problems, often come with high inference costs, and are challenging to design, develop, maintain and scale. — general; rests on literature. [CLM-0017-004]
- Chain-of-Instructions (CoI) prompting, a newly proposed advanced variant of Chain-of-Thought prompting that replaces implicit reasoning directives with a series of explicit instructions directing the model through each stage of a task, seeks to mitigate potential ambiguities and enhance a model's ability to focus on specific aspects of the text by breaking the task into smaller, manageable steps. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0017-005]
- In zero-shot extraction of normative sentences related to § 6 StVO from German court decisions with GPT-4o, Chain-of-Instructions prompting achieved superior Recall and F2 scores compared to Standard, Chain-of-Thought and Layer-of-Thoughts prompting, extracting 108 of 125 gold normative sentences against 99, 102 and 103 respectively. — DE; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0017-006]
- The complexity and nuance of legal language in court rulings makes it challenging for GPT-4o to consistently identify and extract the most relevant normative statements: the model occasionally extracts normative statements beyond the intended scope or includes irrelevant details, and even well-designed standard prompts have not consistently enabled it to interpret the content accurately. — DE; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0017-007]
- Asking GPT-4o to dynamically generate a Chain-of-Instructions prompt from a task description yields inconsistent results — a different prompt structure each time — and lower extraction performance, with the GPT-generated prompts extracting at most 75 of 125 correct normative sentences over three attempts. — DE; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0017-008]
- Breaking a complex extraction task into simpler sub-tasks addressed through the distinct layers of the Layer-of-Thoughts framework allows a large language model to focus on one specific aspect at a time, contributing to higher accuracy, and applying Standard prompts for the first two layers with Chain-of-Instructions prompting for the final layer enhances the framework's effectiveness. — DE; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0017-009]
- Even with moderate precision, automated top-10 extraction of candidate normative sentences that reduces the review workload from 6,242 sentences to 560 would significantly streamline the manual review of court decisions. — DE; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0017-010]
- Applying prompting techniques for legal norm extraction in a zero-shot setting, rather than relying on the intermediate reasoning examples required in few-shot learning, makes the methodology more efficient and easily reusable across similar legal extraction tasks without additional fine-tuning. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0017-011]
- LLM-based extraction of implicit traffic rules from court decisions constitutes a scalable and efficient framework that can be integrated into autonomous vehicle development pipelines, enabling continuous updates to the implicit rule corpus as new judicial decisions are issued. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0017-012]

### Prescriptive

- For evaluating the extraction of normative sentences from court decisions, the F2 measure is preferred because it effectively identifies more relevant sentences, which is crucial for reducing the risk of overlooking any important normative statements. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0017-013]
