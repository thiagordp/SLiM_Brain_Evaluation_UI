# SRC-0014 — ELLA: Empowering LLMs for Interpretable, Accurate and Informative Legal Advice

## What the paper argues

The paper starts from three failure modes of legal large language models with legal article retrieval: retrieval components cannot guarantee complete and clean article sets, so missed articles yield incomplete responses and irrelevant articles bring noise that yields incorrect or inconsistent advice [CLM-0014-001]; the models are sensitive to input perturbation, so identical or slightly varied questions can receive contradictory answers [CLM-0014-002]; and legal terminology may sit in responses without sufficient explanation, hindering users without domain knowledge [CLM-0014-008]. It also identifies a gap: legal case retrieval modules have rarely been integrated into legal-domain LLMs in civil law systems, though relevant cases offer users in-depth reference information [CLM-0014-003]. Its answer is ELLA, a tool around the Lawyer LLaMA consultation model, whose user study grounds four findings: visually presenting the legal-article basis of each response sentence helps users understand and verify the advice, with the basis accurately provided for about 95% of queries [CLM-0014-004]; letting users interactively select the legal articles the model uses improves accuracy, turning largely imperfect top-3 retrievals into correct responses in 80% of cases [CLM-0014-005]; retrieved legal cases with highlighted relevant sentences give comprehensive reference information and improve reading efficiency [CLM-0014-006]; and China's judicial interpretations, shown alongside responses, assist comprehension where interpretation is required [CLM-0014-009]. On the back end, it reports that fine-tuning the BGE embedding model on the LeCaRD dataset significantly improves legal case retrieval over BM25 and untuned BGE, while specialised case retrievers still do better [CLM-0014-007].

## Claims

### Descriptive

- Even when legal large language models are combined with legal article retrieval components, the advice given can still be incorrect or baseless: current legal article retrieval models cannot ensure that all relevant legal articles are retrieved and all irrelevant ones left out, and while missed articles reduce the completeness of responses, retrieved irrelevant articles bring noise that leads the models to produce incomplete, incorrect or inconsistent information. — CN; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0014-001]
- Large language models may be sensitive to input perturbation, so that legal consultation responses can be contradictory when inputs differ only slightly, or even when an identical question is asked in a new conversation; this inconsistency can potentially confuse users and result in a lower-quality consultation. — general; rests on literature. [CLM-0014-002]
- Relevant legal cases can offer users more in-depth reference information when large language models fail to produce coherent and complete responses, yet a legal case retrieval module has rarely been integrated into existing legal-domain large language models in civil law systems. — general; rests on literature. [CLM-0014-003]
- Visually presenting the legal-article basis of each sentence of a large language model's legal-advice response helps users understand the advice and verify its reliability, a sentence lacking any legal basis serving as a warning that it may be incorrect; in a user study the legal article basis of the responses was accurately provided for approximately 95% of queries. — CN; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0014-004]
- Allowing users to interactively select the legal articles a legal large language model uses improves the accuracy and completeness of its responses: in a user study the top three retrieved legal articles were not entirely correct for an average of 83% of queries, but users successfully received correct responses in 80% of cases by selecting relevant legal articles for the model to regenerate its response. — CN; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0014-005]
- Retrieving relevant legal cases and highlighting the sentences related to the user's query provides users with comprehensive reference information in legal consultation: in a user study the legal case retrieval module proved beneficial for 77% of queries on average, and all users agreed that highlighting pertinent sentences significantly streamlines reading the cases and improves reading efficiency. — CN; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0014-006]
- Fine-tuning the general-purpose embedding model BGE on the Chinese legal case retrieval dataset LeCaRD yields a significant increase in each NDCG@K over BM25 and the non-fine-tuned BGE, showing that a fine-tuned embedding model can learn legal knowledge well and better distinguish legal cases that are semantically similar but not relevant in the legal domain, although specialised case-retrieval models such as CaseEncoder, SAILER and CaseFormer still outperform it. — CN; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0014-007]
- Legal terminology may sometimes be embedded in large language models' legal-advice responses without sufficient explanations, posing potential understanding difficulties for users without domain knowledge. — general; rests on literature. [CLM-0014-008]
- Presenting China's judicial interpretations alongside large language model legal-advice responses can assist users in acquiring a better comprehension of the responses when interpretation is required: in a user study roughly 30% of the provided judicial interpretations served to clarify specific legal terminologies or special cases, while for the remaining 70% users claimed they were already familiar with their content. — CN; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0014-009]
