# SRC-0008 — Legal Markup for Reasoning: discursive analysis of tax law court decision texts

## What the paper argues

Against the background that reasoning large language models are developed and evaluated largely in verifiable STEM domains while legal reasoning has received less attention [CLM-0008-003], that existing legal corpora focus on metadata, entities, outcomes or shallow features rather than the discursive structure of legal argumentation [CLM-0008-004], that language models often capture surface patterns rather than the underlying logic of legal reasoning [CLM-0008-005], and that RuTaR is the only known Russian attempt to fill this gap [CLM-0008-006], the paper introduces Legal Markup for Reasoning (LMR), an XML-based discourse annotation scheme inspired by RST but tailored to legal reasoning through stages, roles and function tags [CLM-0008-001]. It finds elementary discourse units ineffective for legal texts and adopts larger single-function fragments [CLM-0008-007]. Automatic annotation with an LLM agrees with experts most on stages (97.31%), moderately on roles and functions [CLM-0008-008], is highly reproducible across runs (Cohen's Kappa 0.82) [CLM-0008-010], while function annotation proves difficult even for human experts (40.65% agreement) because legal texts allow multiple interpretations [CLM-0008-009]. Although Russian procedural law fixes no mandatory template for judicial decisions [CLM-0008-011], an invariant four-stage structure emerges empirically [CLM-0008-012]. The analysis shows that discursive function patterns are subordinate to participant roles [CLM-0008-013], that the Supreme Court's reasoning is the densest and most valuable part for discursive analysis [CLM-0008-014], and that the common legal logic primarily follows the 'cause → legal_ref → conclusion' pattern, with cause as the main connector [CLM-0008-002]; the identified chains are expected to facilitate reasoning-dataset construction and explainability in legal decision prediction [CLM-0008-015].

## Claims

### Descriptive

- Legal Markup for Reasoning (LMR) is an XML-based discourse annotation scheme with a minimal set of attributes that captures the argumentative structure of legal reasoning in tax court decisions; inspired by Rhetorical Structure Theory but substantially modified, it replaces the full RST hierarchy with a minimal set of function tags tailored to legal reasoning, alongside segmentation into text units, participant roles and procedural stages. — RU; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0008-001]
- In tax decisions of the Russian Supreme Court, the common legal logic primarily follows the 'cause → legal_ref → conclusion' pattern — the most frequent three-element function chain — and most discourse functions are linked by the cause function, which confirms its structural importance in legal reasoning as the main connector. — RU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0008-002]
- Reasoning large language models have become a focal point in applied computational linguistics, but their development and evaluation remain largely limited to verifiable domains such as mathematics and programming, while reasoning in the humanities and social sciences, particularly in law, has received less attention. — general; rests on literature. [CLM-0008-003]
- Legal datasets such as LexGLUE, COLIEE and RuTaR support tasks like precedent retrieval and question answering, but existing legal corpora mainly focus on metadata, entities, outcomes or shallow linguistic features rather than the discursive structure of legal reasoning, limiting the analysis of legal argumentation. — general; rests on literature. [CLM-0008-004]
- Specialized legal language models achieve strong results in classification, entity recognition and summarization, and large language models are increasingly used for document generation and legal analysis, but these models often capture surface patterns rather than the underlying logic of legal reasoning, and their ability to perform complex reasoning is still limited. — general; rests on literature. [CLM-0008-005]
- The RuTaR dataset, published in 2025 and based on official correspondence from Russian tax authorities, is designed to capture human-like reasoning and evaluates the reasoning abilities of large language models in the legal field, and is the only known attempt in Russian to address the lack of resources representing reasoning chains in legal texts. — RU; rests on literature. [CLM-0008-006]
- In discourse markup of legal texts, dividing semantic sections into elementary discourse units is ineffective, as it complicates the markup process and makes it more cluttered; identifying larger fragments of text that perform a single function and role — up to entire sentences serving a unified purpose — significantly improves the process and prevents duplication of functions and roles. — RU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0008-007]
- When automatic annotation of Russian court decisions by a large language model (ChatGPT-4.1 with few-shot prompting) is compared with expert annotation, agreement is highest for procedural stages (97.31%), which are marked by clear formal indicators, and moderate for roles (65.59%) and discourse functions (53.76%). — RU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0008-008]
- Annotating discourse functions in legal texts is a difficult task even for experts, because legal texts allow for multiple interpretations: agreement between two independent human annotators on discourse functions was only 40.65%, lower than the AI-human agreement rate of 53.76%. — RU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0008-009]
- Automatic annotation of court decisions by a large language model is highly reproducible: across five iterations on the same pool of texts with identical parameters, average Cohen's Kappa was 0.82 — an almost perfect level of agreement — with average exact match 0.84, unigram Jaccard 0.92 and edit similarity 0.89, and the remaining differences are local in nature. — RU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0008-010]
- There is no single mandatory template for judicial decisions in Russian procedural law: the structure and content requirements for judicial acts are regulated by the Civil Procedure Code of the Russian Federation, the Arbitration Procedure Code and other legal codes, but this does not imply the existence of a rigidly fixed template with immutable fields. — RU; rests on legal sources. [CLM-0008-011]
- Empirical reconstruction of the invariant structure of judicial decisions identifies elements present in the overwhelming majority of texts despite the absence of formal requirements: the stages of preparatory, consideration, debate and decision correspond to the actual structure of Russian judicial acts, even though the law does not require explicit marking of these sections. — RU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0008-012]
- In Russian Supreme Court tax decisions, the distribution of discursive functions is subordinate to the role of the participant in the process: claimants typically initiate disputes through 'elaboration → contrast' sequences, defendants tend to use the 'evidence → fact' pattern, other courts whose decisions are reviewed generally follow the 'legal_ref → cause' logic, and the Supreme Court most often uses the 'contrast → legal_ref' pattern. — RU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0008-013]
- In Russian Supreme Court tax decisions, parts of the text relating to the Supreme Court have on average the highest number of units per document, suggesting that the court's reasoning has a more 'dense' logical structure; the court's arguments are the most valuable part for discursive analysis, as they contain the most meaningful fragments of reasoning. — RU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0008-014]

### Predictive

- Identifying the stable discursive patterns that constitute the logic of legal court decision texts will facilitate the construction of reasoning datasets in legal and other non-STEM domains, and lays a foundation for improving the explainability of large language models in legal decision prediction; the identified function chains can serve as building blocks enabling the automatized reconstruction of court decisions. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0008-015]
