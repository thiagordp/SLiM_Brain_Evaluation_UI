# SRC-0013 — The Privacy Quagmire: Where Computer Scientists and Lawyers May Disagree

## What the paper argues

The paper diagnoses a fundamental gap between legal language and code verification: four challenges — vague language, evolving terminology, exception recognition and external dependencies — act as roadblocks to fully automated privacy compliance checking [CLM-0013-001]. Privacy policies use intentionally flexible terms with no computational definition, while formal verification requires precise predicates [CLM-0013-002]; real policies encode legal requirements, business practices and user expectations that resist simple formalization [CLM-0013-003]; and regulations like the GDPR and the CCPA compound the problem with jurisdiction-specific requirements [CLM-0013-004]. Existing tools fail on novel concepts because of fixed taxonomies [CLM-0013-005], misread coherent exception patterns as contradictions [CLM-0013-006], cannot resolve external legal and implementation dependencies [CLM-0013-007], and rely on parsers that break on syntactic variation or force vague terms into predefined categories, unlike LLMs [CLM-0013-008]. Its answer is to embrace ambiguity: an LLM extracts sender, receiver, data subject, data type, action and condition from each policy statement, the elements are encoded in first-order logic, and vague terms are preserved as uninterpreted predicates for human review [CLM-0013-009], while Contextual Integrity transmission principles are left to human judgment [CLM-0013-010]. Empirically the pipeline extracted 974 and 3,801 data practice edges from the TikTok and Meta privacy policies [CLM-0013-011]; embedding-based semantic search shrinks each verification query by over 99%, yielding zero timeouts across 23 queries with sub-linear scaling [CLM-0013-012], since full-policy FOL formulas remain too complex for SMT solvers [CLM-0013-013]. The paper concludes that formalizing legal text has both promise and fundamental limits: formal methods should identify clear-cut issues while human expertise resolves genuine ambiguities [CLM-0013-014].

## Claims

### Descriptive

- Four fundamental challenges act as roadblocks to making fully automated privacy compliance checking feasible: vague terms with no computational definition, evolving terminology that does not fit predefined categories, exception patterns that appear contradictory to automated tools, and external legal dependencies not defined in the policy text. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0013-001]
- Privacy policies use terms, such as 'business operations', that have no computational definition; these terms are intentionally flexible to cover future business needs, whereas formal verification requires precise predicates. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0013-002]
- Real privacy policies address broader concerns than data confidentiality: they encode legal requirements about appropriate data use, business practices, and user expectations that resist simple formalization. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0013-003]
- New regulations such as the GDPR and the CCPA compound the difficulty of translating vague privacy policy terms into concrete access control rules by adding jurisdiction-specific requirements that change how data can be processed. — EU+US; rests on legal sources. [CLM-0013-004]
- Existing automated privacy policy analysis tools rely on fixed categorization schemes that fail when policies mention novel concepts, and attempting to dynamically expand taxonomies risks creating overlapping categories that break the logical consistency needed for formal reasoning. — general; rests on literature. [CLM-0013-005]
- Privacy policies state general rules and then carve out specific exceptions, which appear contradictory to automated tools that treat each statement independently; humans understand that the later statement creates a specific exception to the general rule, but current analyzers struggle to recognize the hierarchical relationship where specific rules override general ones, and manual review shows that most apparent contradictions in policies are actually coherent exception patterns. — general; rests on literature. [CLM-0013-006]
- Privacy policies reference external context that is not defined within the policy text, such as which laws apply in each jurisdiction or how an application actually implements its settings; formalizing these statements requires information beyond the policy text itself, and even entity-sensitive analysis cannot determine which specific laws trigger sharing in which contexts. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0013-007]
- Grammar-based parsers fail when semantically equivalent privacy policy phrases differ in syntax, and neural parsers would force vague terms into predefined categories; large language models, by contrast, can interpret varied expressions of the same concept and extract semantic roles while preserving vague terms for human interpretation. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0013-008]
- Full 'transmission principles' in the sense of Contextual Integrity - the complex social norms governing when data sharing is appropriate - are not extracted automatically from privacy policies because they require human judgment about social context and significant human interpretation. — general; rests on literature. [CLM-0013-010]
- An LLM-based extraction pipeline processed the privacy policies of TikTok (approximately 15,000 words) and Meta (over 40,000 words), extracting 974 and 3,801 distinct data practice edges respectively, demonstrating scalability from shorter to more comprehensive privacy documents and revealing complexities such as multi-actor data flows and individually trackable data types expanded from enumerated lists. — undetermined; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0013-011]
- Embedding-based semantic search reduces each verification query over a privacy policy's extracted data practice edges to a small relevant subset - on average 6.4 edges for the TikTok policy and 18.5 for the Meta policy, a 99.38% and 99.50% reduction in the verification problem - enabling tractable formal reasoning by an SMT solver: 23 queries of varying complexity achieved zero timeouts with average query times of 3.39s and 3.91s, and although the Meta policy is 3.9 times larger than the TikTok policy, query times increased by only 1.15 times, demonstrating sub-linear scaling behavior. — undetermined; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0013-012]
- The first-order logic formulas produced from full privacy policies remain too complex for SMT solvers: without a semantic search phase reducing the search space, a query would require the SMT solver to reason about thousands of disjunctive clauses, which leads to exponential complexity. — undetermined; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0013-013]

### Prescriptive

- Privacy policy formalization should embrace rather than abstract away the limitations of legal language: a large language model can identify six key elements of each policy statement - the data sender, receiver, data subject, data type, action performed, and any conditions - and the extracted elements can be encoded as first-order logic, while vague terms are preserved as uninterpreted predicates rather than defined, making the ambiguity explicit for human review. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0013-009]
- Formalizing legal privacy policy text shows both promise and fundamental limits; rather than attempting full automation, privacy policy analysis should be structured so that formal methods identify clear-cut issues while human expertise resolves genuine ambiguities. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0013-014]
