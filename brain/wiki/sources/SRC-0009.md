# SRC-0009 — Automated Extraction of Judicial Interpretative Formulas in EU Case Law on VAT

## What the paper argues

Starting from the observation that the CJEU argues through broad interpretative statements — Judicial Interpretative Formulas, developed through self-citation and distinct from ratio decidendi [CLM-0009-002] — and that VAT, harmonised only partially through a Directive, has had its substance progressively shaped by CJEU rulings [CLM-0009-003], the paper proposes the first pipeline for automatically extracting JIFs from CJEU VAT decisions [CLM-0009-001]. Against the drawbacks of existing automated principle-extraction approaches — costly annotation for data-driven methods, unstable and opaque LLMs [CLM-0009-004] — it defines JIFs operationally through annotation guidelines with the paragraph as unit [CLM-0009-005], which yield almost perfect inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's kappa 0.96) [CLM-0009-006], and contributes a corpus of 101 CJEU preliminary rulings split so that manually annotated documents serve evaluation and LLM-annotated ones serve training [CLM-0009-014]. DeepSeek-R1, the best of three LLM annotators evaluated, labelled the training split [CLM-0009-007]. BERT-based models fine-tuned on this LLM-annotated data perform comparably to LLMs (macro F1 up to 0.76) [CLM-0009-008], plausibly inheriting the annotator's tendency to favour false positives [CLM-0009-009]; a LinearSVC comes close, suggesting lexical cues are crucial [CLM-0009-010]. LEGAL-BERT is considered the best model, its high recall being what legal practitioner tools need [CLM-0009-011]. The paper concludes that LLM-produced annotations can distil LLM knowledge into smaller, more transparent and reproducible models, reducing annotation costs [CLM-0009-012], against the backdrop of LLMs' proprietary nature, opacity and hallucinations in the legal domain [CLM-0009-013].

## Claims

### Descriptive

- Manually extracting Judicial Interpretative Formulas (JIFs) from decisions of the Court of Justice of the European Union on Value Added Tax is effortful, automatic extraction had not previously been investigated in the VAT domain, and a pipeline method for automated JIF extraction in this domain is the first of its kind. — EU; rests on literature. [CLM-0009-001]
- Because VAT harmonisation was implemented through a Directive that regulates only certain aspects and leaves others to national discretion, numerous inconsistencies have emerged over time; consequently the interpretation of VAT rules has frequently fallen to the CJEU, whose rulings have progressively shaped the substance of VAT law. — EU; rests on legal sources. [CLM-0009-003]
- Approaches to automated extraction of legal principles face two main drawbacks: data-driven methods require costly annotation, while LLM-based methods often lack stability, transparency, and reproducibility, which are essential in the legal domain. — general; rests on literature. [CLM-0009-004]
- Annotation guidelines for Judicial Interpretative Formulas in CJEU VAT decisions, refined over successive annotation stages, yield almost perfect inter-annotator agreement: Cohen's kappa, measured on an independently annotated document set at paragraph level and restricted to the relevant portions of the judgments, was 0.96. — EU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0009-006]
- Among LLMs evaluated with a guideline-based prompt for paragraph-level annotation of Judicial Interpretative Formulas against a manually annotated validation split of CJEU VAT decisions — Gemini-1.5-pro, DeepSeek-R1 and Claude-3.7-sonnet — DeepSeek obtained the highest score and was therefore adopted to annotate the training documents. — EU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0009-007]
- BERT-based architectures fine-tuned on LLM-annotated training data perform comparably to LLMs in classifying paragraphs of CJEU VAT decisions as containing Judicial Interpretative Formulas: the highest macro F1 score of 0.76 was achieved by both DistilRoBERTa and LEGAL-BERT, with all other models closely behind at a minimum of 0.72, comparable to or even better than DeepSeek. — EU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0009-008]
- Annotations produced automatically by LLMs can be exploited to distil LLM knowledge into much smaller models that obtain comparable results, reducing annotation costs and improving scalability; using a dedicated task-specific classifier for the final extraction of Judicial Interpretative Formulas combines the strengths of LLM prompting with a more transparent and reproducible model, mitigating LLM limitations at deployment. — EU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0009-012]
- A general limitation of using LLMs in the legal domain lies in their proprietary nature and lack of transparency, as well as susceptibility to hallucinations. — general; rests on literature. [CLM-0009-013]
- A novel corpus for Judicial Interpretative Formula extraction consists of 101 CJEU preliminary rulings on VAT — on the subtopics of taxable amounts and exemptions for the public interest, retrieved through a concept-based EUR-Lex search in December 2024 — structured into three document-level splits that separate manually annotated data for evaluation and development (21 expert-labelled decisions) from automatically annotated data for training (80 LLM-labelled decisions), with the test split containing only documents never used during guideline development. — EU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0009-014]

### Interpretative

- The Court of Justice of the European Union adopts an argumentative style with broad interpretative statements — Judicial Interpretative Formulas, legal texts or standards the CJEU develops over time through self-citation — whose value extends beyond specific issues as they are progressively cited and integrated into EU law; these differ from ratio decidendi in that they are not limited to the elements necessary to reach the Court's conclusion. — EU; rests on literature. [CLM-0009-002]
- For annotation purposes a Judicial Interpretative Formula may be defined as an interpretative statement by the Court, either formulated for the first time or drawn from cited case law; the paragraphs identified as JIFs are those containing interpretation of a rule or general principle, consequences stemming from its interpretation or application, subsumption of a fact within a rule, or qualification of a fact as a concept contained within a rule, and the annotation unit is one paragraph, reflecting the typical structure of CJEU judgments, organised in numbered paragraphs each expressing an autonomous step of reasoning. — EU; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0009-005]
- Transformer models trained on data annotated by DeepSeek exhibit behaviours similar to generative LLMs — higher recall and lower precision on the positive class — and it is reasonable to speculate that they may have learned similar patterns and a tendency to favour false positives over false negatives. — EU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0009-009]
- In classifying CJEU decision paragraphs as containing Judicial Interpretative Formulas, a LinearSVC with TF-IDF features performs not much inferior to state-of-the-art Transformer models, suggesting that lexical cues play a crucial role in the task. — EU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0009-010]
- LEGAL-BERT may be considered the best model for Judicial Interpretative Formula extraction: it is the most stable and has the best macro F1 score and the best F1 score on the positive class, and its near-best recall on the positive class is particularly relevant for tools intended for legal practitioners, since the presence of additional JIFs is preferable to the absence of fundamental ones — users can easily discard a few irrelevant paragraphs, but cannot know if a crucial JIF is missing. — EU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0009-011]
