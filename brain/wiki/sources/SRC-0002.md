# SRC-0002 — Evaluating Open and Proprietary Large Language Models in Law Interpretation: The Case of the EU VAT Directive

## What the paper argues

The paper reports an exploratory case study in which nine proprietary and open large language models from the Claude, GPT, Mistral and Llama families answered 19 legal questions of varying complexity on the EU VAT Directive, with expert-rated average truthfulness scores between 7.4 and 8.6, indicating overall strong performance [CLM-0002-001], addressing a gap in the literature regarding model efficacy in specialized legal domains [CLM-0002-010]. The two GPT-4 variants scored best, Llama 3.1 405B third, and Llama 3.1 8B and Mistral Large lowest [CLM-0002-002]; Llama 3.1 405B performing similarly to the GPT-4 models and significantly better than Llama 3.1 8B indicates that scaling significantly improves accuracy [CLM-0002-003], while Llama 3.1 8B's variable but sometimes very good scores indicate potential for improvement through fine-tuning with domain-related data [CLM-0002-004]. Models from the same family agree more with one another than with models from other families, possibly mirroring common architectures and training methodologies [CLM-0002-005], the highest agreement (Cohen's Kappa 0.65) being between GPT-4o and Llama 3.1 405B [CLM-0002-006]. Some questions draw more disagreement across models, suggesting they are harder or more ambiguous, while two were answered with the top score by all models [CLM-0002-007]; on questions representing exceptional cases in the law, models do not align well on the correct response, emphasizing the need to assess robustness [CLM-0002-008]. The paper concludes that standardized evaluation benchmarks across model families are needed and that human oversight and domain-specific fine-tuning remain crucial where consistency is essential, such as the legal domain [CLM-0002-009], and that enhancing open models with fine-tuning or retrieval-augmented generation is a promising direction for legal use cases [CLM-0002-011].

## Claims

### Descriptive

- In an exploratory case study in which nine proprietary and open large language models from the Claude, GPT, Mistral and Llama families answered 19 legal questions of varying complexity on the EU VAT Directive, average truthfulness scores as rated by a legal expert fell between 7.4 and 8.6, indicating overall strong performance. — EU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0002-001]
- On expert-scored truthfulness in answering legal questions about the EU VAT Directive, the two GPT-4 variants (GPT-4 Turbo and GPT-4o) achieved the best scores, Llama 3.1 405B the third highest, while Llama 3.1 8B and Mistral Large achieved the lowest scores. — EU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0002-002]
- Llama 3.1 405B performs similarly to the GPT-4 large language models and significantly better than Llama 3.1 8B on truthfulness in answering questions on the EU VAT Directive, with clearly improved and more stable responses, indicating that scaling significantly improves the accuracy of results. — EU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0002-003]
- Large language models from the same family show increased agreement scores in their answers to EU VAT Directive questions compared to the agreement between models from different families, possibly mirroring the anticipated impact of common architectures, training methodologies, or similar optimization processes. — EU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0002-005]
- The highest agreement between large language models answering EU VAT Directive questions, a Cohen's Kappa coefficient of 0.65, was observed between GPT-4o and Llama 3.1 405B, meaning that these two models have a similar behavior in producing accurate responses. — EU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0002-006]
- Certain EU VAT Directive questions show more disagreement across large language models, suggesting they may be harder or more ambiguous to answer, while two questions achieved the highest score across all models, which may indicate that they are straightforward and less complex for the models to answer. — EU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0002-007]
- When answering questions that represent exceptional cases in the law, large language models do not align well on providing the correct response, which emphasizes the need to assess specific aspects of trustworthiness such as robustness — how well a model responds to questions regarding exceptional cases — in tasks like law interpretation. — EU; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0002-008]
- There is a gap in the literature regarding the efficacy of large language models in specialized legal domains, which a comparison of model performance in interpreting the EU VAT Directive addresses. — general; rests on literature. [CLM-0002-010]

### Prescriptive

- Because different large language models produce varying responses, there is a need to create and use standardized evaluation benchmarks across model families, and human oversight and domain-specific fine-tuning remain crucial in applications where consistency is essential, such as the legal domain. — general; rests on factual basis. [CLM-0002-009]
- Enhancing open large language models by applying methods like fine-tuning or retrieval-augmented generation is a promising direction for making them more effective and better suited to legal use cases. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0002-011]

### Predictive

- Llama 3.1 8B faces increased variation in truthfulness across legal questions on the EU VAT Directive but achieved very good scores on some questions, indicating a potential for improvement, for example by fine-tuning it using domain-related data to enable more accurate responses. — EU; rests on factual basis.
