# SRC-0016 — Preparing AI for Compliance: Initial Steps of a Framework for Teaching LLMs to Reason About Compliance

## What the paper argues

The paper starts from the position that the rapid integration of large language models into applications faces significant challenges from the complexity of global regulatory and ethical frameworks such as the GDPR and the AI Act [CLM-0016-001], and holds that leveraging LLMs to explain, review, and assess AI models, datasets, and pipelines against legislation, regulations, ethical guidelines, and social impact can help address those challenges [CLM-0016-002]. Any general compliance-assistant solution will likely require retrieval-augmented generation in which the model reasons over the specific retrieved compliance requirements applying to a product, service, or company at a given time in a given jurisdiction [CLM-0016-003], and this differs from existing legal AI assistants and benchmarks in that the user selects which provisions are considered by selecting what can be retrieved [CLM-0016-009]. Because annotating compliance reasoning data demands expensive legal expertise [CLM-0016-004], the authors propose a tool that automates the generation of compliance reasoning data, with a refinement process so only the best candidates reach the annotators, aiming to facilitate AI-driven compliance assistants [CLM-0016-007]. Methodologically, they hold that many RAG pipelines' uniform-length chunking disregards document structure and risks critical oversight with legal texts organized into articles and paragraphs [CLM-0016-005], whereas their structure-preserving RAG method — articles and paragraphs, then overlapping passages for dense retrieval — ensures accurate retrieval and contextually aware reasoning [CLM-0016-006]. They conclude that integrating a compliance assistant into the AI development process can let companies proactively ensure compliance, catch legal issues early, and reduce manual legal reviews [CLM-0016-008].

## Claims

### Descriptive

- The rapid integration of large language models into diverse applications faces significant challenges due to the complexity of global regulatory and ethical frameworks, such as those in the GDPR and the AI Act, and diverse, evolving regulatory landscapes across countries challenge developers, data scientists, researchers, regulators, and policymakers. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0016-001]
- Annotating and labeling data for teaching and evaluating a compliance assistant demands the expertise of legal professionals to ensure accuracy, making the process both time-consuming and expensive. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0016-004]
- A common limitation of many retrieval-augmented generation pipelines is their disregard for the structural integrity of documents, often dividing them into uniform-length chunks, which can lead to critical oversight, especially when dealing with legal documents, which are typically organized into articles and paragraphs. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0016-005]
- A retrieval-augmented generation method that preserves the structure of legal texts — dividing a legal document into its pre-defined articles and paragraphs and segmenting each paragraph into overlapping passages for dense retrieval — ensures accurate retrieval and interpretation of relevant provisions and more accurate, contextually aware reasoning. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0016-006]
- Reasoning on the compliance of AI tools with variable provisions differs from existing legal AI assistants and legal-reasoning benchmarks: given a large language model instructed to reason only on specific retrieved provisions, the user can select which provisions are considered by selecting those that can be retrieved, for example only laws that apply in the EU, plus provisions applying to the financial sector, plus the user's own ethical guidelines. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0016-009]

### Predictive

- Leveraging large language models to explain, review, and assess AI models, datasets, and complete pipelines from the perspective of legislations, regulations, ethical guidelines, and social impact can help address the challenges posed by rapid technological advancement and diverse, evolving regulatory landscapes. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0016-002]
- Any general solution for an AI compliance assistant will likely require some form of retrieval-augmented generation in which the large language model can reason over the specific set of retrieved compliance requirements that can apply to a single product, service, or company at a given point in time within a certain jurisdiction. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0016-003]
- A tool that automates the generation of compliance reasoning data — selecting real-world examples of AI technologies and explaining how specific legal and ethical guidelines impact them, followed by a refinement process so that only the best candidates are presented to annotators — aims to facilitate the development of AI-driven compliance assistants that can effectively align with global legal and ethical standards. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0016-007]
- By integrating a compliance assistant into the AI development process, companies can proactively ensure that their models and data pipelines comply with complex regulations, identify potential legal issues early in the development cycle, and streamline the process by reducing the need for extensive manual reviews by legal experts, thereby reducing compliance risks and accelerating time-to-market. — general; rests on abstract considerations. [CLM-0016-008]
